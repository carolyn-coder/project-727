Paul’s calls

Five decisions shape the next twelve months, and two of them block everything behind them. For each one we have named what we would do and what it costs to wait. Mark where Paul lands and we will build against it.

How this works. Approving a recommendation here tells us to proceed on that basis. Nothing is locked until a vendor contract is signed, and any of these can change by telling us.

Blocking now

01

Stock components or custom tooling

Blocking

The briefs ask for a tinted-opaque atomizer, a hinge-free squeeze tube, and a no-glass pump. Those are specific, but they are not exotic. Stock suppliers can match all three off the shelf. Custom-molded tooling would give the line a shape nobody else has, at a cost of two to four months and roughly $20,000 per mold amortized across the run.

Plan + Pivot recommendationGo with stock components. This is the single biggest lever on the entire calendar, and custom tooling costs more time than the whole plan has in cushion. Revisit custom for the second wave, when volume makes the tooling pay for itself.

Cost of waiting: nothing can be quoted or booked until this lands. The packaging partner has been introduced and needs this to quote. If custom turns out to be non-negotiable, the July 2027 date does not hold and we should know that in August, not in December.

Needed by Aug 31, 2026 Not yet decided
02

Target cost and annual volume per SKU

Blocking

All three product briefs leave Quantity, Annual Volume, and Target Cost blank. No fragrance house, packaging supplier, or manufacturer will quote without them. This is the true critical-path item, and it is the one we cannot answer because it depends on how much inventory risk Paul wants to carry on a first run.

Plan + Pivot recommendationStart at 7,500 units per product. IPG confirmed on August 11 that they run 5,000 to 10,000 units for a new brand and that 25,000 is their typical run, so 7,500 sits inside the window they already work in. Every cost figure in this portal is built on it. It is enough to get real pricing without committing to inventory the demand has not been proven for.

Cost of waiting: every week this sits, Phase 1 slides a week, and Phase 1 has no slack behind it.

Needed immediately Not yet decided
03

The Perfume Mist: concentration, or price, or neither

Blocking

The Perfume Mist brief benchmarks against Scentual's confirmed 50% fragrance load. That is true parfum strength. A body mist normally runs 15% to 20%. At 50% on a prestige-tier compound, the oil alone costs $20.70 a unit and the finished product lands at $27.43 against a $24 price. Three good options: dial the concentration back, lift the price, or run the Perfume Mist deliberately as a hero that pulls people into the other two products.

Plan + Pivot recommendationDial to 20% and hold $24. That cuts roughly $12 to $15 off the unit and puts the Perfume Mist at a healthy margin without touching the price positioning. At 20% it is still meaningfully stronger than most body mists on shelf, so the "higher concentration" promise in the brief survives.

Cost of waiting: concentration drives the fragrance house brief, and Custom Essence is waiting on a brief right now. Settling it before the brief goes out saves a re-brief and an iteration cycle.

Needed by Aug 31, 2026 Not yet decided

Right behind those

04

Where the line sells first

Soon

Direct-to-consumer first, or wholesale from day one. Prestige retail sell-in cycles run twelve to twenty-four months on their own, which does not fit inside this runway alongside building the product.

Plan + Pivot recommendationLaunch direct first, but build wholesale-ready packaging from day one, meaning UPC codes and proper case packs. Then pursue retail sell-in starting in year two. The packaging decision is nearly free now and expensive to retrofit later.

Cost of waiting: low for a few weeks, then it starts constraining packaging specs in Phase 3.

Needed by Aug 31, 2026 Not yet decided
05

Domestic or overseas sourcing

Soon

Overseas suppliers are cheaper per component. They also add four to eight weeks of freight and customs on top of production lead time, and this calendar has about six weeks of total cushion. IPG runs six factories, all domestic: one in Southern California, three in Texas, one in New Mexico, and one in Tennessee. Engaging them as the product arm largely answers this question already.

Plan + Pivot recommendationConfirm domestic through IPG’s own factory network. The per-unit saving overseas does not cover the schedule risk on a first run with a fixed launch date attached to it, and IPG’s footprint makes domestic the path of least resistance rather than a compromise. The open question is now which of their six sites fits each product, and California is their boutique site for hand-finished, intricate packaging.

Cost of waiting: low now that IPG is engaged. Worth confirming on the record so packaging and fill are quoted against the right sites.

Needed by Aug 31, 2026 Not yet decided

Settled

06

The vegan claim

Decided · the line will be vegan

All three IPG briefs asked the same question in the same words: vegan is not required, help us understand the implication. Settled August 12. The line will be formulated and certified vegan, which is the right call and lines up with what the category is asking for. IPG’s own read is that shoppers now expect vegan as a baseline in this space.

What this changes, and whenThe named ingredients across the three briefs, bacuri butter, castor oil, coconut oil, and glycerine, are all plant-derived, so the formulas start from a good place. The work is in what gets excluded and proven: beeswax, honey, lanolin, carmine, silk, collagen, and animal-derived glycerine all come off the table, and every raw material needs a supplier declaration on file.

The near-term action: this belongs in the fragrance brief going to Custom Essence. Several classic fragrance materials are animal-derived, including civet, castoreum, ambergris, and beeswax absolute, and synthetic equivalents exist for all of them. Naming the constraint now costs nothing; discovering it after two rounds of samples costs an iteration cycle.

Certification to be selected · claims lock with labels, Feb 2027 Decided Aug 12

Later, but on the radar

Strategy

Shop-by-routine and hero-product pairings both showed up as proven bundle patterns in the competitive sweep, and Sol de Janeiro leans on them hard. The Perfume Mist, Body Melk, and Shower Oil already form a natural morning-to-finish routine.

Plan + Pivot recommendationLaunch them as a set from day one, with bundle pricing and routine-based merchandising, rather than three products competing for separate cart adds. It also gives the Perfume Mist a job beyond its own margin.

Our lane is product. Product development, merchandising and assortment strategy, sourcing, planning, and command of the numbers. Brand and marketing sit with Miss C-Suite. No overlap, on purpose.

What the monthly retainer covers

  • Product development management. Concept to production-ready, managing development partners and the testing and compliance process, structured so Paul owns or exclusively holds the signature scents and formulas.
  • Sourcing and supply chain. Vendor identification and negotiation, bills of materials, landed-cost models, production and lead-time planning.
  • Assortment, pricing, and financial planning. Buy planning and open-to-buy, margin validation at the agreed price point, limited-edition strategy, launch and first-year financial modeling.
  • Channel and distribution strategy. Where and how the line sells, line sheets and cost sheets, launch logistics alongside Miss C-Suite.
  • Production and launch management. Purchase orders placed and tracked, production and quality milestones monitored.
  • Post-launch learnings. First month after launch, early results reviewed and translated into the next planning cycle.
  • Education as we go. The reasoning behind every recommendation, so Paul finishes this build with more command of the business than he started with.

What sits outside our scope

  • Brand identity, creative, content, social, and marketing execution, owned by Miss C-Suite
  • Fragrance creation, formulation, and laboratory testing, performed by appointed fragrance houses and labs. We manage and coordinate these partners, we do not do lab work.
  • Manufacturing, fill, and fulfillment, performed by appointed suppliers
  • Legal, regulatory, and tax filings, and final regulatory sign-off. You are the responsible party. We coordinate and flag.
  • Photography, website build, storefront setup, and paid media
  • Capital, inventory purchases, and third-party costs Paul funds

Testing and compliance

Body care goes on skin, so we treat this as a managed workstream rather than an afterthought. Working with qualified labs we anticipate stability and compatibility testing, preservative efficacy and microbial testing, a safety assessment, and a regulatory and claims review. For a fragrance-forward line we also plan for fragrance allergen screening, IFRA compliance, and skin patch testing. With the vegan claim now decided, we add certification and the supplier declarations that substantiate it to the same workstream.

Depending on ingredients and channels this can extend to heavy metal and impurity screening, pH and shelf-life substantiation, and transit and packaging testing for online orders. We map the path for federal cosmetics registration and product listing, and for California Proposition 65.

Testing is performed by qualified third-party labs and billed at cost. We manage the process, the timeline, and the documentation. As brand owner Paul holds final responsibility for regulatory compliance, registration, and claims, with our coordination and guidance.

What we need from Paul

  • A single empowered decision maker for timely approvals. Today that is Paul, with Jack coordinating.
  • Funding for third-party development, testing, and production costs, and the budget to meet the calendar.
  • Timely access to the data, systems, decisions, and people the work needs.

Before any third-party work begins we provide an estimated budget by category with a not-to-exceed amount for each, so Paul approves the full program cost before we commit a dollar.

Ownership

On full payment Paul owns the final deliverables created for the brand: the briefs, plans, models, and calendars. Where the line includes original fragrances and formulations, we structure the agreements with fragrance houses and labs so that wherever possible Paul owns or exclusively controls the signature scents and formulas developed for the brand.

The gap. Across all twelve brands patterned, nobody occupies founder-led scent authenticity, true gourmand originality, and real accessibility at the same time. The road to launch

Launching July 2027. Industry benchmarks put a genuinely new fragrance platform, meaning new formulas across three forms with real packaging and real safety testing, at ten to thirteen months using stock components and a stock-base-plus-custom-fragrance approach. Fourteen to twenty months if custom tooling or overseas sourcing enters the picture. Twelve months is achievable with the sequencing below.

How we protect the date. We fast-track the creative side, since the concepts, competitive research, and scent direction are already done. And we hold firm on the two things that genuinely cannot be rushed: safety and stability testing, and the packaging tooling decision.

Where the cushion sits

The timing cushion sits in two places: eighteen days between the internal July 12 target and the one-year mark, and six weeks between finished goods landing on May 31 and that internal target.

Testing is the one phase with a fixed length. Stability and preservative-efficacy work takes eight to fourteen weeks by its nature, and protecting that window is what keeps the launch clean. If formulation runs long, that time comes out of the cushion above rather than out of testing.

The componentry choice is the one to settle early. Custom packaging tooling adds two to four months, which is more than this cushion carries. Settling it in August is what keeps every date behind it intact.
